AI providers and models: who makes what, and how to choose

A handful of companies train the leading models; you can reach most of them through their own API, through the big cloud platforms, or, for open-weight models, by downloading and running them yourself. Line-ups change every few months, so every fact below carries the date it was checked: 2026-10-28. Treat the names as a snapshot and confirm on the linked page before you build.

Two families are hard to place in one row:

  • Licences differ by model, not by company. Mistral uses Apache 2.0 for some models and a Modified MIT licence for others; Meta's Llama licences are Meta's own, not a standard open-source licence. Read the licence of the exact model you plan to use, on its model card.
  • "Open" means open weights, not open training data. You can download and run the model; you usually cannot see or reproduce how it was trained.

Closed vs open-weight models

A closed model runs only on its maker's servers (or a cloud partner's) and you pay per token; an open-weight model you can download and run wherever you like, paying for the hardware instead.

Closed (API) Open-weight
Access API key, per token Download, for example from the Hugging Face Hub
Where data goes To the provider (see data protection) Wherever you run it, including your own servers
Effort None beyond the API call You host it, on GPUs for larger models (see the cloud)
Customizing Prompts; fine-tuning only where offered Fine-tuning and modification, within the licence
Who else serves it The maker and its cloud partners Many hosts offer open models by API too

You can also use open-weight models without running them: many providers and cloud platforms serve them by API. Mistral's own platform, for example, lists third-party models such as DeepSeek V4.1 Flash alongside its own (Mistral models, checked 2026-10-28).

Where you can get them

Most leading models are available both from their maker and from at least one large cloud platform, which matters if your company already buys from that cloud. For example, Anthropic's models page lists IDs for the Claude API, Amazon Bedrock, Claude Models on Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry and Claude Platform on AWS (Anthropic models overview, checked 2026-10-28). Amazon Bedrock offers "access to hundreds of top foundation models" from several makers (Bedrock models at a glance, checked 2026-10-28). Microsoft lists "Foundry Models sold by Azure", including OpenAI's (Microsoft Learn, checked 2026-10-28).

The same model on two platforms can differ in price, regions, features, data terms and retirement dates. Anthropic notes that partner platforms "set their own retirement schedules" (Anthropic model deprecations, checked 2026-10-28).

How model names and versions work

A model name usually combines a family, a generation number and a tier; in production, send a fixed model ID and upgrade on purpose. Tiers, snapshots, aliases and retirement dates are explained on Model names and versions.

How to choose

Start from your task and your constraints, test two or three candidates on your own examples, and pick the cheapest one that passes. Public rankings help you shortlist; only your own tests tell you which is good enough for you.

  1. Constraints first. Must the data stay in the EU, or on your servers? Does your company already buy from one cloud? Do you need an open licence? These can eliminate most options before any test. See data protection.
  2. Shortlist by tier, not brand. For classification and extraction, try the small fast tiers first. For long agentic coding or hard reasoning, the large tiers. Anthropic's own page, for instance, recommends its smallest current model "For high-volume, latency-sensitive tasks such as classification, extraction, and routing" (models overview).
  3. Test on 20 to 50 real examples from your app, with a clear pass/fail check.
  4. Compare cost per task, not per token. A cheaper model that needs two retries may cost more. Prices are on each vendor's pricing page (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Mistral).
  5. Check latency if users wait for the answer: time to first token and total time.
  6. Keep switching cheap. Wrap the model call in one function so a later change is one line.

Public comparisons (useful for shortlisting, each measuring something different):

  • Arena ranks models from blind human votes between two anonymous answers; "Only votes made while the models are anonymous count toward official rankings" (checked 2026-10-28).
  • Artificial Analysis benchmarks "intelligence, quality, performance, and price", including speed and latency (checked 2026-10-28).
  • The Hugging Face Hub is where most open-weight models are published, with their model cards and licences.
